AEGIS — How It Works

An interactive model of the operating architecture. Three layers, one decision loop, two operating modes. Switch modes, run the loop, and watch where each part sits and what it decides.

NSF SBIR/STTR Phase I Project Pitch · companion to PROJECT-PITCH-v0.7-SUBMISSION.md · internal review draft, not for submission · v1.0

1 · The one thing

A smart city collects data. An intelligent city can be trusted to act.

AEGIS is a decision layer for a water utility. It sits across a utility's existing systems — SCADA, smart meters, GIS, work orders — and does the one thing they cannot: decide what to do next, and prove the decision was right. It decides by rehearsing rather than improvising.

2 · The stack — what sits on top of what

Hover a layer to isolate it
Layer 4 · Decide

Agents — Admiral + specialists

Supervisory Admiral over specialists (water, weather & hazard, compliance, assets, cost). Sees only the trust-weighted picture, resolves conflicts between specialists, exchanges structured information rather than conversational messages. Specialists act, not merely report, through explicit authority envelopes and an auditable log.

Deterministic · rule-bounded · approval gates
Layer 3 · The four speeds

Rehearsal engine

Holds a library of scenario archetypes grounded in real events (freeze-thaw main-break seasons, flood, HazMat release, cascading outage) and replays them continuously against live operational data — asset registry, crew roster, traffic, weather, inventory — scoring each candidate response plan against a human-planning baseline.

The research contribution · the falsifiable claim
Layer 2 · Understand

Ontology + trust

Zone mesh — a two-tier spatial-semantic map (service zones over semantic zones) with learned routing preferences between zones, weighted by inverse hop count. Data-trust scoring — every feed carries a decaying score for how much to believe it right now, and the weighting adapts to regime: stable → speed-of-reply, volatile → freshness, unknown → blend.

Bounded · interpretable · decays
Layer 1 · Connect

Swarm federation fabric

Each node keeps a small local table of pheromone scores for its neighbours — trust, latency, reliability, right now — learned passively from who actually answers. When a message must travel more than one hop, a node selects no more than one neighbour to pass it to; the rest stay quiet.

The transport layer · patented US 7,957,355 B1
Normal conditions

The agentic ontological layer runs on the utility's existing central systems. The swarm fabric federates the fragmented edges — the systems nobody integrated.

What changes in grid-down. The fixed tier — pumps, treatment, SCADA — dies with the grid. The surviving mobile tier forms a MANET: crews, tankers and inspectors talking directly, with no central platform, no fixed power and no fixed infrastructure. The swarm fabric establishes it. The agentic layer does not switch off and start improvising — it runs on that MANET, against the scenarios already rehearsed and the trust scores already learned. Authority envelopes and human gates are unchanged.

3 · The decision loop

Six verbs. This is the whole system running end to end.

STEP 1
Sense
Pull the live picture: SCADA, AMI, GIS, work orders, leak log, weather, traffic, crew roster, contractor status.
✓
STEP 2
Analyse
Trust-score every feed for right now. Build the zone picture. Label each fact with how fresh it is and how much to believe it.
✓
STEP 3
Decide
Rehearse: match the situation against the scenario library, synthesise candidate plans, score each against the human-planning baseline.
✓
STEP 4
Authorise
Check the plan against the authority envelope. Low consequence → proceed. High consequence → a human approves. Stale plans are refused, not executed.
✓
STEP 5
Act
Dispatch the crew, raise the work order, order the parts. The swarm fabric carries every message — and routes around whatever link is down.
✓
STEP 6
Verify
Compare the real outcome to the rehearsed plan. Feed the residual back into the zone field, the trust scores and the library.
✓
Idle — press run

What each layer contributed
LayerContribution
1 · Fabric Work order reaches the crew, the parts store and the contractor — routed around a dead zone at the north end rather than failing.
2 · Ontology + trust The leak-logger feed has been answering slowly for 20 minutes → its trust score has decayed → it is weighted down. The SCADA pressure feed is fresh → weighted up. That changes the zone picture.
3 · Rehearsal Freeze-thaw archetype replayed against today's crews, today's traffic, today's weather. Plan B beats the human baseline on time and cost.
4 · Agents Water specialist produces the plan; assets specialist confirms the isolation valve and the excavator; the Admiral resolves the conflict between them. High-consequence step → human approval.
Note what the swarm did NOT do. It did not decide which crew to send. It did not prioritise Zone 3 over Zone 5. It did not choose the repair method. One crew repairs one leak. What the swarm made possible is that the crew, the work order, the parts inventory, the leak log and the contractor all stayed in one federated picture — which is precisely what the utility's own consultant found missing.

7 · The four speeds — and why the transitions are the hard part

A sensing-and-actuation system has only four possible states, and almost every deployed system sits in exactly one. The transitions between them are where both the value and the risk live.

NOW · rehearse

Rehearse

Scenario library + replay harness + plan synthesis, scored against a human-planning baseline.

Scenario generation from live state
ONSET · recognise

Recognise

Scenario-match detector; confidence weighted by trust scores.

Match a rehearsed plan — one engine, many hazards
EVENT · act

Act

Pre-computed plan executor: envelope check → approval gate → execute → audit.

Binding problem, not a reasoning problem
AFTER · learn

Learn

Outcome reconciliation → residual analysis → field, trust and library update.

The rehearsal library is itself adaptive
The architecture rule — learning where safe, deterministic where lethal. Pheromone fields, trust scoring and zone learning are online, bounded, interpretable and decaying. Forecasting is narrow supervised ML, forecast-only. Everything that acts — dispatch, routing, action selection, plan execution — is deterministic and rule-bounded, because in a disaster distribution shift is extreme and reinforcement learning hallucinates-optimises exactly where provable correctness is required.

AEGIS uses learning where it is safe and deterministic reasoning where it is lethal. The action layer is deliberately not an AI model improvising in a crisis.

8 · What this does not do

We do not claim
  • To keep fixed infrastructure alive through a grid collapse. Pumps, treatment and SCADA die with the grid.
  • To predict novel disasters. It rehearses grounded scenario archetypes.
  • To replace the utility's control centre. The swarm earns its place at the edges and in grid-down operation.
  • To swarm-dispatch crews. One crew repairs one leak.
  • To have better leak detection. Detection is solved; the gap is the decision after it.
  • To hold any ISAC IP.
What it does do
  • Prove, with a score, that a rehearsed decision beats a human-planned one.
  • Codify a utility's undocumented operating procedures into something executable and measurable.
  • Keep crew, work order, parts, leak log and contractor in one federated picture.
  • Keep answering when the network breaks — routing around gaps rather than going blind.
  • Refuse a stale plan rather than execute it.
  • Show a number, including where it loses.

9 · Glossary

Swarm federation fabricThe transport layer. Every node keeps a small scorecard of its neighbours and picks one to pass messages to. No central routing table. Patented — US 7,957,355 B1.
Pheromone scoreA decaying reputation score on a connection — built up when that neighbour answers reliably, fading when it doesn't. It scores data and links, never vehicles or people.
Zone meshTwo-tier map: geographic zones (service areas, corridors, feeders) over semantic zones (asset class, owning organisation, criticality).
Destination-zone pheromoneA learned score for "who is the best next hop for reaching Zone X", weighted by inverse hop count.
Data-trust scoringA live number for how much to believe a given feed right now. Adapts to regime: stable → speed-of-reply, volatile → freshness, unknown → blend.
MANETMobile Ad-hoc NETwork — devices that form a network directly with each other, with no central server. What the surviving mobile tier becomes when the grid goes down.
RehearsalReplaying a scenario against live data to produce and score a response plan, before it is needed.
Scenario archetypeA reusable class of event grounded in real history — freeze-thaw main-break season, flood, HazMat release, cascading outage.
Authority envelopeThe defined limits of what the system may do without asking a human.
Approval gateThe point where a high-consequence action must be signed off by a person.
Human-planning baselineWhat an experienced operator would have done. The score that makes the claim falsifiable.
SCADA / AMI / GISSupervisory control · smart meters reporting hourly · the map database of which pipes are where.
